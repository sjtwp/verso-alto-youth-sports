Beat the Heat: Arizona Hydration and Heat Safety Tips for Young Athletes

SafetyJuly 21, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

Arizona is a wonderful place to raise active kids — but our heat is no joke. Even in spring and fall, temperatures in the Phoenix area can climb quickly. That's one reason we plan our games for Saturday mornings, when it's cooler. Here are practical tips every East Valley sports family should know.

1. Start hydrating the day before

Hydration isn't just about the water bottle at the game. Encourage your child to drink water throughout the day before practices and games. A well-hydrated child handles heat much better.

2. Bring more water than you think

Send a large, insulated water bottle — frozen halfway the night before works great in Arizona. Kids should take water breaks often, even if they don't feel thirsty.

3. Protect against the sun

  • Apply sunscreen 15–30 minutes before arriving and reapply as directed.
  • Light-colored, breathable clothing helps keep kids cooler.
  • Bring a pop-up canopy or umbrella for the sideline if you can.

Read our full guide to sun safety for kids in Arizona.

4. Know the warning signs

Watch for heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps, unusual tiredness, or flushed skin. If you see these signs, get your child into shade, give them water, and cool them down. If symptoms are severe — such as confusion, fainting, or hot, dry skin — call 911 right away.

5. Fuel up with smart snacks

Water-rich fruits like oranges and watermelon are perfect sideline snacks. See our list of healthy game-day snacks (and please remember to be allergy-aware — our participation agreement explains how we handle food allergies).

Our commitment

Player safety comes first at Verso Alto. Coaches will build in regular water breaks and adjust activity based on conditions. Questions? Visit our FAQ or contact us.
