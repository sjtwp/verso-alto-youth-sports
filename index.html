Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc. · Last Updated: November 14, 2026

1. Introduction

Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc. (“Verso Alto Youth Sports,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) is a nonprofit organization dedicated to providing affordable, faith-centered youth sports programs that foster faith, family, teamwork, and sportsmanship.

We respect the privacy of our participants and their families and are committed to protecting the personal information entrusted to us.

This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, store, share, and protect information obtained through our website, registration forms, payment systems, and league activities.

2. Information We Collect

We may collect the following information when families register for programs, volunteer, make donations, or communicate with Verso Alto Youth Sports.

Parent and Guardian Information

  • Full name
  • Email address
  • Phone number
  • Mailing address, when necessary
  • Emergency contact information
  • Registration and communication preferences

Participant Information

  • Child's full name
  • Date of birth
  • Age and applicable program division
  • Relevant medical conditions, food allergies, or other safety considerations
  • Emergency contact information
  • Parent or guardian authorizations and acknowledgments

Registration and Payment Information

  • Program registrations and associated fees
  • Payment status and transaction records
  • Donation information
  • Information necessary for Arizona Empowerment Scholarship Account (ESA) transactions, invoices, or receipts

Payments may be processed through third-party payment providers, including Stripe. Verso Alto Youth Sports does not intentionally collect or store complete payment card numbers through its registration records.

Volunteer Information

For coaches and other volunteers, we may collect contact information, volunteer preferences, training acknowledgments, and information necessary to administer background screening requirements.

Photographs and Videos

Photographs and videos may be captured during league activities for authorized organizational, promotional, and communication purposes, consistent with applicable parent/guardian acknowledgments and restrictions.

3. How We Use Information

Information collected by Verso Alto Youth Sports may be used to:

  • Process registrations and administer youth sports programs.
  • Organize teams, schedules, rosters, and league communications.
  • Contact parents and guardians regarding activities, schedule changes, and important announcements.
  • Maintain participant safety and respond to medical emergencies.
  • Process payments, donations, refunds, and ESA-related documentation.
  • Coordinate coaches, volunteers, and background screening requirements.
  • Maintain organizational, accounting, and compliance records.
  • Share authorized photographs, videos, and organizational updates.
  • Improve the operation and accessibility of our programs.

We will not use children's personal information for unrelated commercial marketing.

4. Children's Privacy

Verso Alto Youth Sports serves children and recognizes the importance of protecting their personal information.

All program registrations must be completed by a parent or legal guardian.

Our website and registration services are intended for use by adults, not for independent submission of personal information by children under age 13.

We collect children's information from parents or guardians for legitimate registration, program administration, and safety purposes.

Parents and guardians may contact Verso Alto Youth Sports to request access to, correction of, or deletion of their child's information, subject to applicable legal, safety, accounting, and recordkeeping requirements.

5. Sharing of Information

Verso Alto Youth Sports does not sell or rent personal information.

We may share limited information when reasonably necessary with:

  • Authorized coaches and volunteers who require information to perform their responsibilities.
  • Payment processors, including Stripe.
  • Technology providers supporting registration, communication, recordkeeping, and website operations.
  • Background screening providers for applicable volunteer screening.
  • ESA administrators or payment platforms when necessary to process eligible transactions.
  • Medical professionals or emergency responders when necessary to protect a participant's health or safety.
  • Government authorities, insurers, or other parties when required by law or reasonably necessary for legal compliance and organizational protection.

Access to participant information will be limited to individuals with a legitimate organizational need.

6. Medical and Sensitive Information

Information regarding allergies, medical conditions, emergency contacts, and other participant safety concerns is collected to help provide a safe environment.

Such information will be shared only with authorized individuals who reasonably need it for participant supervision, emergency response, or program administration.

Verso Alto Youth Sports will take reasonable measures to safeguard sensitive participant information and will not publicly disclose medical information without appropriate authorization or another lawful basis.

7. Payment Processing and Donations

Verso Alto Youth Sports may use third-party services, including Stripe, to process registration payments, donations, and recurring contributions.

Payment providers maintain their own privacy and security practices.

Families and donors are encouraged to review the applicable provider's privacy policies.

Verso Alto Youth Sports may retain payment confirmations, transaction identifiers, donation records, invoices, and other information necessary for financial administration and compliance.

8. Photography and Video

Verso Alto Youth Sports may photograph or record league activities for purposes such as program updates, website content, social media, and community outreach.

Photography and video practices will follow the organization's applicable registration acknowledgments and media policies.

Parents or guardians with questions or concerns regarding the use of their child's image may contact Verso Alto Youth Sports.

We will review reasonable requests regarding identifiable images of children, subject to applicable permissions, existing publications, and practical limitations.

9. Data Storage and Security

Verso Alto Youth Sports uses reasonable administrative, technical, and organizational safeguards intended to protect personal information against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, or loss.

These measures may include restricted account access, password protection, and the use of established third-party technology providers.

No electronic system or internet transmission can be guaranteed completely secure.

10. Data Retention

Verso Alto Youth Sports retains personal information only as long as reasonably necessary for program administration, participant safety, financial recordkeeping, legal compliance, insurance requirements, or other legitimate organizational purposes.

Information that is no longer reasonably necessary will be deleted, anonymized, or securely disposed of when practical and consistent with applicable retention obligations.

11. Website Services and Third-Party Links

Our website may use services provided by Google and other third-party technology providers.

Our website may also contain links to external websites, including payment processors and other service providers.

Verso Alto Youth Sports is not responsible for the privacy practices of independently operated third-party websites.

Those services may collect information according to their own privacy policies.

12. Parent and Guardian Rights

Parents and legal guardians may contact Verso Alto Youth Sports to:

  • Request access to personal information submitted about themselves or their children.
  • Request corrections to inaccurate information.
  • Request deletion of information, where permitted.
  • Ask questions about information sharing or privacy practices.
  • Raise concerns regarding the use of photographs or videos.

Verso Alto Youth Sports may request reasonable verification of parental or guardian authority before responding to requests involving a child's information.

13. Changes to This Privacy Policy

Verso Alto Youth Sports

Registration & Refund Policy

Verso Alto Youth Sports is committed to providing an affordable, organized, and enjoyable youth sports experience. Registration fees help cover field rentals, equipment, uniforms, insurance, and other program expenses.

Participant Withdrawal & Refunds

Refunds for participant withdrawals are determined by when a written cancellation request is received:

  • Before the registration deadline: A full refund will be issued.
  • After the registration deadline, but before the season begins: A 50% refund will be issued.
  • Once the season has begun: No refunds will be issued.

All cancellation requests must be submitted in writing to shostutler@versoaltoyouthsports.com.

Program Cancellation

If Verso Alto Youth Sports cancels an entire program or season, registered families will receive a full refund.

Missed Games or Practices

Refunds will not be provided for individual games or practices missed due to personal scheduling conflicts, illness, or other participant absences.

Weather & Unforeseen Circumstances

Games or practices may be canceled or rescheduled due to weather, field conditions, or circumstances beyond the organization's control.

Verso Alto Youth Sports will make reasonable efforts to provide makeup opportunities when possible. Refunds will not be issued for individual cancellations when reasonable efforts have been made to accommodate the schedule.

ESA Payments

Registrations paid through an Education Savings Account (ESA) will be refunded in accordance with applicable ESA program requirements and payment processing procedures.

Policy Administration

Refund requests will be reviewed and processed in accordance with this policy and the organization's financial procedures.

Verso Alto Youth Sports reserves the right to consider exceptional circumstances on a case-by-case basis, consistent with applicable requirements.

Concussion Awareness & Player Safety Policy

← All policies & forms

Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc.

Applies To: All participants, parents/guardians, coaches, volunteers, and athletic programs operated by Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc.

1. Purpose

Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc. (“Verso Alto Youth Sports”) is committed to providing a safe, positive, and faith-centered athletic environment for all children.

The purpose of this policy is to promote concussion awareness, establish appropriate procedures for responding to suspected head injuries, and prioritize the health and well-being of every participant.

2. Understanding Concussions

A concussion is a traumatic brain injury caused by a bump, blow, or jolt to the head or body that causes the brain to move rapidly within the skull.

Concussions may occur without a loss of consciousness, and symptoms may not appear immediately.

Possible signs and symptoms include:

  • Headache or pressure in the head
  • Dizziness, balance difficulties, or unusual clumsiness
  • Confusion, memory problems, or difficulty concentrating
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Blurred vision or sensitivity to light or noise
  • Unusual fatigue, irritability, or changes in behavior
  • Appearing dazed, stunned, or unusually quiet

For younger children who may have difficulty describing symptoms, coaches and parents should watch for unusual crying, changes in behavior, loss of interest in activities, or difficulty walking or maintaining balance.

3. Immediate Removal From Play

Any participant who exhibits signs or symptoms of a concussion, or who is suspected of sustaining a concussion, must be immediately removed from all athletic activities.

The participant may not return to practice, competition, or other athletic activities on the same day, even if symptoms appear to resolve.

Coaches and volunteers must prioritize player safety over athletic participation.

When in doubt, the participant will be removed from play.

4. Parent/Guardian Notification

When a concussion is suspected, a coach or authorized league representative will:

  • Immediately remove the participant from athletic activity.
  • Notify the participant's parent or legal guardian as soon as reasonably possible.
  • Recommend prompt evaluation by an appropriate licensed healthcare professional.
  • Document the incident and notify Verso Alto Youth Sports leadership.

A participant with a suspected concussion must not be left unattended while awaiting appropriate care or transfer to a parent/guardian or medical professional.

5. Emergency Medical Situations

Emergency medical services (911) must be contacted when a participant experiences potentially serious symptoms, including:

  • Loss of consciousness or difficulty waking
  • Seizures or convulsions
  • Repeated vomiting
  • Increasing confusion, agitation, or unusual behavior
  • A worsening or severe headache
  • Weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking
  • Unequal pupils or double vision
  • Significant difficulty walking or maintaining balance

Coaches and volunteers should not attempt to move a participant when a serious neck or spinal injury is suspected, except when necessary to prevent immediate danger.

6. Medical Evaluation and Return to Play

Any participant removed from activity because of a suspected concussion must receive an appropriate medical evaluation before returning to athletic participation.

A participant may return to athletic activity only when:

  • The participant has been evaluated by an appropriate licensed healthcare professional.
  • Written medical clearance authorizing a return to athletic participation has been provided to Verso Alto Youth Sports.
  • The participant has completed any recommended recovery or graduated return-to-play process.
  • The parent or legal guardian has been informed of and supports the participant's return.

A parent or guardian's permission alone does not replace required medical clearance.

Verso Alto Youth Sports reserves the right to delay a participant's return if additional safety concerns remain.

7. Coach and Volunteer Responsibilities

Coaches and volunteers are expected to:

  • Maintain awareness of concussion signs and symptoms.
  • Promptly remove participants with suspected concussions.
  • Notify parents/guardians and league leadership of suspected injuries.
  • Follow established emergency procedures.
  • Never pressure or encourage an injured participant to continue playing.
  • Follow applicable league safety requirements and age-appropriate rules.

Coaches and volunteers are not expected to diagnose concussions or provide medical treatment beyond their training.

8. Parent/Guardian Responsibilities

Parents and legal guardians are expected to:

  • Inform coaches of known injuries, concussions, or relevant medical restrictions.
  • Monitor their children for symptoms following a suspected head injury.
  • Seek appropriate medical evaluation when a concussion is suspected.
  • Follow medical recommendations regarding recovery and activity restrictions.
  • Provide required written medical clearance before their child returns to play.

9. Age-Appropriate Safety Measures

Verso Alto Youth Sports recognizes that younger children may be particularly vulnerable to injury and may have difficulty communicating symptoms.

The organization will promote age-appropriate instruction, safe playing environments, appropriate supervision, and rules designed to reduce unnecessary head contact.

For youth soccer, deliberate heading will not be taught or permitted for participants age 10 and younger.

Coaches should emphasize safe play, proper technique, and good sportsmanship.

10. Incident Documentation

Suspected concussions and significant head injuries must be reported to Verso Alto Youth Sports leadership and documented in an incident report.

Incident documentation should include the date, location, circumstances of the injury, observed symptoms, actions taken, and parent/guardian notification.

Medical information and incident records will be handled confidentially and shared only as reasonably necessary for participant safety, administration, insurance, or legal compliance.

11. Policy Compliance

All coaches, volunteers, parents, and participants are expected to cooperate with this policy.

Verso Alto may restrict participation when necessary to protect the health and safety of a participant.

The organization may update this policy to reflect changes in applicable laws, medical guidance, insurance requirements, or league operations.

Child Protection & Safe Environment Policy

← All policies & forms

Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc.

Applies To: All directors, officers, coaches, assistant coaches, volunteers, employees, contractors, and other individuals serving in roles involving contact with minors through Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc.

1. Purpose

Verso Alto Youth Sports, Inc. (“Verso Alto Youth Sports”) is committed to providing a safe, welcoming, and faith-centered environment where children can grow in character, friendship, sportsmanship, and love of God.

The safety, dignity, and well-being of every child entrusted to our programs are of the highest importance.

This policy establishes standards and procedures intended to prevent abuse, neglect, misconduct, and inappropriate behavior while promoting accountability among all individuals involved in Verso Alto Youth Sports activities.

Verso Alto Youth Sports maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward child abuse, exploitation, harassment, and intentional misconduct involving minors.

2. Scope and Responsibilities

This policy applies to all Verso Alto Youth Sports-sponsored practices, games, events, meetings, and other activities involving minors.

All covered individuals are responsible for:

  • Protecting the physical and emotional well-being of participants.
  • Maintaining appropriate boundaries with minors.
  • Following established supervision and safety procedures.
  • Promptly reporting suspected abuse, neglect, or misconduct.
  • Cooperating with investigations and corrective actions.
  • Treating all participants and families with dignity and respect.

3. Background Checks and Volunteer Screening

All adult coaches, assistant coaches, and other adults serving in positions involving regular interaction with or responsibility for minors must complete an approved criminal background screening before beginning their duties.

Verso Alto Youth Sports may require background checks for additional positions based on the nature of the individual's responsibilities.

Screening requirements include:

  • Completion of a volunteer application or registration.
  • Disclosure of relevant criminal history or disqualifying circumstances.
  • Successful completion of an organization-approved background check.
  • Agreement to follow Verso Alto Youth Sports policies and standards of conduct.

Background checks must be renewed at least every two years, or more frequently if required by law, insurance, or an applicable governing organization.

Verso Alto Youth Sports reserves the right to deny, suspend, or terminate volunteer participation based on screening results or other credible safety concerns.

A background check does not replace ongoing supervision or adherence to this policy.

4. Safe Environment Training

Verso Alto Youth Sports may require all adult coaches and volunteers who supervise or regularly interact with minors to complete child protection and safe environment training approved by Verso Alto Youth Sports before assuming their responsibilities.

Training should address:

  • Recognizing signs of child abuse and neglect.
  • Appropriate adult-child boundaries.
  • Safe supervision practices.
  • Responding to suspected abuse or misconduct.
  • Reporting responsibilities.
  • Appropriate communication with minors and families.

Verso Alto Youth Sports may recognize equivalent training completed through a Catholic diocese, parish, school, or other approved youth-serving organization, provided it meets the organization's requirements.

Training must be renewed as determined by Verso Alto Youth Sports and any applicable governing requirements.

5. Adult Supervision and the Two-Adult Standard

Verso Alto Youth Sports will make reasonable efforts to ensure that at least two screened and approved adults are present during organized activities involving minors.

Adults must avoid isolated, one-on-one situations with unrelated minors.

When two approved adults are not available:

  • Activities should remain in open, observable areas.
  • Parents or guardians should remain nearby or be asked to assist with supervision.
  • Coaches must avoid private or secluded interactions with children.
  • The activity may be modified, delayed, or canceled when adequate supervision cannot be maintained.

An adult must never arrange a private, isolated meeting with an unrelated minor through a Verso Alto program.

6. Appropriate Conduct and Physical Contact

Coaches and volunteers must model Christian values, good sportsmanship, patience, and respect.

Appropriate conduct includes:

  • Using encouraging and age-appropriate language.
  • Treating children fairly and respectfully.
  • Providing instruction without humiliation or intimidation.
  • Maintaining professional and appropriate physical boundaries.
  • Using physical contact only when reasonably necessary for safety, instruction, or age-appropriate encouragement.

Prohibited conduct includes:

  • Physical, sexual, or emotional abuse.
  • Corporal punishment or physical discipline.
  • Bullying, hazing, harassment, threats, or intimidation.
  • Sexualized language, jokes, gestures, or inappropriate conversations.
  • Unnecessary or inappropriate touching.
  • Favoritism intended to create secrecy, dependence, or inappropriate relationships.
  • Possession or use of alcohol, illegal drugs, or impairing substances while supervising children.
  • Any behavior that places a participant at unreasonable risk.

7. Restrooms, Changing Areas, and Personal Assistance

Coaches and volunteers should encourage children to use restroom facilities with assistance from their own parent or legal guardian whenever practical.

Adults must not enter a restroom or changing area alone with an unrelated child except when an immediate safety concern requires intervention.

When assistance is necessary:

  • A parent or guardian should be contacted whenever practical.
  • Another approved adult should be present or within sight or hearing.
  • The child's privacy and dignity must be respected.
  • Assistance should be limited to what is reasonably necessary.

Verso Alto Youth Sports does not permit adults to photograph or record children in restrooms or changing areas.

8. Transportation and Release of Participants

Parents and legal guardians are generally responsible for transporting their children to and from Verso Alto Youth Sports activities.

Coaches and volunteers should not transport unrelated minors individually in personal vehicles as part of league activities without prior authorization from Verso Alto Youth Sports leadership and the child's parent or guardian.

Children must be released only to their parent, legal guardian, or another individual authorized by the parent or guardian.

Coaches must not knowingly leave a child unattended following an organized activity.

9. Communication With Minors

Communication regarding Verso Alto Youth Sports activities should ordinarily occur directly with parents or legal guardians.

Coaches and volunteers should use organization-approved communication channels whenever available.

Private electronic communication between an adult volunteer and an unrelated minor is prohibited unless necessary for an immediate safety concern.

When communication with a minor is necessary, a parent or guardian or another approved adult should be included.

Coaches and volunteers must not establish inappropriate private relationships with participants through social media, text messaging, or other communication platforms.

10. Photography, Video, and Participant Privacy

Photography and video recording may occur during Verso Alto Youth Sports activities in accordance with the organization's registration agreements, media acknowledgments, and privacy practices.

Coaches and volunteers must:

  • Respect applicable parental restrictions and organizational media policies.
  • Avoid photographing children in private or sensitive situations.
  • Refrain from sharing identifying or sensitive participant information without authorization.
  • Use photographs and videos only for appropriate organizational purposes.

11. Recognizing and Reporting Suspected Abuse

All individuals covered by this policy must take suspected child abuse, neglect, exploitation, or serious misconduct seriously.

When abuse or neglect is suspected:

  • Ensure immediate safety. If a child is in immediate danger, call 911.
  • Report to the appropriate authorities. Any individual who is required to report under Arizona law must promptly make a report to law enforcement or the Arizona Department of Child Safety, as applicable. Verso Alto Youth Sports requires its personnel to promptly report reasonable suspicions of child abuse to the appropriate authorities, even if their individual legal reporting status is uncertain.
  • Notify Verso Alto Youth Sports leadership. Inform the President/Executive Director or another uninvolved board officer as soon as reasonably possible, without delaying a required report to authorities.
  • Preserve relevant information. Document observations, statements, dates, and circumstances accurately.
  • Avoid independent investigations. Do not interrogate the child, confront the accused, or attempt to determine whether an allegation is true.

An internal report to Verso Alto Youth Sports does not replace any legally required report to public authorities.

Individuals should not promise absolute confidentiality to a child making a disclosure.

12. Response to Allegations and Misconduct

When Verso Alto Youth Sports receives a credible allegation or reasonable suspicion of abuse, exploitation, or serious misconduct involving a covered individual, the organization will take appropriate protective measures.

These may include:

  • Immediately restricting or suspending the individual's involvement with minors while concerns are assessed.
  • Cooperating with law enforcement and child protective authorities.
  • Preserving relevant records and communications.
  • Notifying the Board of Directors as appropriate.
  • Taking disciplinary or administrative action consistent with applicable law and organizational policy.

Verso Alto Youth Sports will not retaliate against any person who makes a good-faith report of suspected abuse or misconduct.

13. Parent and Guardian Responsibilities

Parents and legal guardians are important partners in maintaining a safe environment.

Parents are encouraged to:

  • Review league safety policies.
  • Communicate relevant safety or supervision concerns.
  • Ensure appropriate drop-off and pickup arrangements.
  • Report suspected misconduct promptly.
  • Reinforce respectful behavior and appropriate boundaries with their children.

Parents are welcome to observe ordinary practices and games, subject to reasonable field access and safety rules.

14. Policy Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in corrective action, suspension, removal from volunteer responsibilities, termination of involvement, or referral to appropriate authorities.

Verso Alto Youth Sports reserves the right to take immediate protective action when necessary to safeguard participants.

15. Policy Review and Updates

The Board of Directors will review this policy at least annually and may update it as necessary to reflect changes in applicable laws, insurance requirements, child protection guidance, and organizational operations.

All covered individuals are expected to comply with the most current approved version.

Choosing Soccer Cleats and Shin Guards for Young Kids

SoccerNovember 10, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

You don't need expensive gear for youth soccer. Here's what actually matters for players ages 3–8.

Shin guards (required)

  • Choose a size based on your child's height — check the manufacturer's chart.
  • Slip-in guards with ankle protection are easy for little ones.
  • Guards should cover most of the shin, from just below the knee to above the ankle.

Cleats or athletic shoes

  • For the youngest players, comfortable athletic shoes work fine.
  • If you buy cleats, choose molded rubber cleats designed for grass.
  • Leave about a thumb's width of room at the toe.
  • Velcro straps are great for kids who can't tie laces yet.

Socks

Tall soccer socks hold shin guards in place and protect skin from turf and sun.

Save money

Kids grow fast! Hand-me-downs, swaps with other families, and secondhand sports stores in the East Valley are great options.

Concussion Awareness for Youth Sports Parents

SafetySeptember 15, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

Head injuries are uncommon in young recreational soccer, but every parent should know the basics. Here's a quick guide based on our Concussion Awareness & Player Safety Policy.

What is a concussion?

A concussion is a brain injury caused by a bump, blow, or jolt to the head or body. It can happen without a loss of consciousness, and symptoms don't always show up right away.

Signs to watch for

  • Headache or pressure in the head
  • Dizziness or balance problems
  • Confusion or trouble concentrating
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Sensitivity to light or noise
  • Unusual tiredness, irritability, or behavior changes

Younger children may not be able to describe how they feel. Watch for unusual crying, loss of interest in play, or trouble walking.

"When in doubt, sit them out"

Under our policy, any player with a suspected concussion is removed from play immediately and does not return that day. Parents are notified, and a licensed healthcare professional should evaluate the child.

Returning to play

A player may return only after written medical clearance and any recommended recovery steps. Parent permission alone isn't enough — safety comes first.

Age-appropriate play

For youth soccer, deliberate heading is not taught or permitted for players age 10 and younger in our programs.

Monsoon Season and Outdoor Sports: Arizona Weather Safety for Families

SafetySeptember 8, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

Arizona's monsoon season brings dramatic skies, much-needed rain — and real safety concerns for outdoor activities. While our first soccer season is in the spring, monsoon awareness is important for every Arizona sports family.

Lightning: when thunder roars, go indoors

If you hear thunder, lightning is close enough to be dangerous. Players and families should leave the field and take shelter in a building or hard-topped vehicle. Wait until the storm has clearly passed before returning.

Dust storms (haboobs)

Dust storms can roll across the Valley quickly, reducing visibility and air quality. If one approaches, head indoors or to your car. If you're driving, follow the "Pull Aside, Stay Alive" guidance from Arizona transportation officials.

Heat and humidity

Monsoon humidity can make heat feel even more intense. Review our heat safety tips and keep water close.

How we handle weather changes

Games or practices may be canceled or rescheduled due to weather, field conditions, or circumstances beyond our control. We'll make reasonable efforts to provide makeup opportunities. Our refund policy explains how weather cancellations work.

Stay informed

Make sure your contact info is up to date when you register so you get schedule changes quickly. Have questions? Contact us or visit the FAQ.

  • Apply before leaving home so it has time to absorb.
  • Don't forget ears, necks, the backs of knees, and the tops of feet.
  • Reapply after sweating or as directed on the product.

Dress for the desert

Lightweight, light-colored clothing reflects heat. Hats with brims protect faces on the sideline, and UV-protective sunglasses can help between games.

Shade is your friend

Pop-up canopies and umbrellas give players a place to cool down between drills. Kids should rest in the shade during breaks whenever possible.

Timing matters

The sun is strongest in the middle of the day. That's why Verso Alto plans games for Saturday mornings — cooler temperatures, softer light, and happier kids.

Sun and heat go hand in hand. Read our full heat safety and hydration guide and our game-day packing list.

What's the Best Age to Start Soccer? A Guide for Arizona Parents

SoccerJuly 28, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

One of the most common questions we hear from East Valley parents is, "Is my child old enough for soccer?" The short answer: if your child is 3 or older and loves to run, they're probably ready.

Ages 3–4: first touches

At this age, soccer is all about fun and movement. Kids learn to kick, stop the ball, follow simple directions, and play alongside other children. Don't worry about rules — the goal is joy and confidence. Expect lots of laughing, a few distracted moments, and plenty of high-fives.

Ages 5–6: building skills

Kids this age start dribbling with purpose, passing to teammates, and understanding the idea of a team. Small-sided games keep everyone involved and touching the ball often.

Ages 7–8: game time

By 7 and 8, players are ready for positions, simple tactics, and more structured games. They also start to understand sportsmanship more deeply — winning and losing with grace.

Signs your child is ready

  • They can follow simple, two-step instructions.
  • They enjoy being active and playing with other kids.
  • They can be separated from a parent for short periods (a parent nearby is always welcome!).

Why rec soccer is a great starting point

Beat the Heat: Arizona Hydration and Heat Safety Tips for Young Athletes

SafetyJuly 21, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

Arizona is a wonderful place to raise active kids — but our heat is no joke. Even in spring and fall, temperatures in the Phoenix area can climb quickly. That's one reason we plan our games for Saturday mornings, when it's cooler. Here are practical tips every East Valley sports family should know.

1. Start hydrating the day before

Hydration isn't just about the water bottle at the game. Encourage your child to drink water throughout the day before practices and games. A well-hydrated child handles heat much better.

2. Bring more water than you think

Send a large, insulated water bottle — frozen halfway the night before works great in Arizona. Kids should take water breaks often, even if they don't feel thirsty.

3. Protect against the sun

  • Apply sunscreen 15–30 minutes before arriving and reapply as directed.
  • Light-colored, breathable clothing helps keep kids cooler.
  • Bring a pop-up canopy or umbrella for the sideline if you can.

Read our full guide to sun safety for kids in Arizona.

4. Know the warning signs

Watch for heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps, unusual tiredness, or flushed skin. If you see these signs, get your child into shade, give them water, and cool them down. If symptoms are severe — such as confusion, fainting, or hot, dry skin — call 911 right away.

5. Fuel up with smart snacks

Water-rich fruits like oranges and watermelon are perfect sideline snacks. See our list of healthy game-day snacks (and please remember to be allergy-aware — our participation agreement explains how we handle food allergies).

Our commitment

Player safety comes first at Verso Alto. Coaches will build in regular water breaks and adjust activity based on conditions. Questions? Visit our FAQ or contact us.

What "Verso l'Alto" Means — and Why We Named Our League After It

FaithJuly 14, 2026· 2 min read· By Verso Alto Youth Sports

When people hear our name for the first time, they often ask: what does "Verso Alto" mean? The answer is at the very heart of who we are.

"To the heights"

Verso l'alto is Italian for "to the heights." The phrase is closely associated with St. Pier Giorgio Frassati, a young Italian man who loved the mountains, loved his friends, and loved God with his whole heart. He wrote the words on a photograph from one of his climbs, and they've since become a rallying cry for young Catholics around the world.

Who was Pier Giorgio Frassati?

Pier Giorgio was an athlete, an outdoorsman, and a devoted friend. He was known for his joy, his generosity to the poor, and his deep prayer life. He showed that holiness isn't boring or distant — it's lived out in friendships, adventure, and everyday kindness. That's exactly the example we want to set for kids on the soccer field.

What it means for young athletes

For a 5-year-old learning to dribble, "to the heights" might mean trying again after a missed kick. For an 8-year-old, it might mean helping a teammate up or shaking hands after a tough game. We want every child to learn that striving for greatness includes effort, humility, and love for others — not just winning.

Mountains in our own backyard

Here in the East Valley, we're surrounded by mountains: the Superstitions, the San Tans, and Usery. It feels fitting that a league inspired by a mountain-loving saint would take root among Arizona's peaks. It's why our logo and website feature a rising sun over the mountains — a reminder of the early Saturday-morning games our families will share.

Living it out

Our programs will include prayer, encourage good sportsmanship, and celebrate effort over scoreboards. Read more about our mission, explore our sports, or see how we approach building character through sports.

St. Pier Giorgio Frassati, pray for us. Verso l'alto!
